About the Planner

An unofficial helper for getting around Tokyo by subway: the 9 Tokyo Metro lines and the 4 Toei lines. Enter your location by GPS, address or station, choose a destination, and get a step-by-step guide covering which line to take and in which direction, where to change trains, and what the fare comes to.

How route finding works

Location → Best Station
The Haversine formula gives the distance from your GPS position or address to every station. The planner tries the nearest few (up to 5 within walking range) and picks whichever gets you there soonest once walking time is counted, so a slightly further station on the right line beats an extra change.
Dijkstra's Shortest Path
Each line's stop at a station is its own node, so changing trains has a real cost: the walk between platforms (longer at sprawling stations like Otemachi or Shinjuku) plus an average wait. Connected stations with different names, such as Akasaka-mitsuke and Nagatacho, are linked by their transfer passages. Dijkstra's algorithm then finds the quickest journey, with a small extra penalty per change to prefer direct routes.
Line and Direction
Tokyo signs send you to a platform by line and direction (“Ginza Line for Asakusa”), not by platform number, so that's what each step shows, along with the station code (G09 and so on). The Oedo Line runs in a loop, so its direction is given as the next major stations, as on its platform signs. Many lines run through onto other railways, so the train itself may display a destination beyond the end of the subway line.

Fares

Tokyo Metro and Toei are separate operators, each with its own distance-based fare. A journey using both is charged both fares minus a ¥70 connecting discount. IC card (Suica / PASMO) fares are charged to the yen; paper tickets round up to ¥10. Children pay half.

Good to know

Hours
Trains run from about 5:00 until shortly after midnight. Last trains vary by line and station, so check the timetable posted in the station if you're travelling late.
Data and accuracy
Station order and positions come from OpenStreetMap route relations (checked October 2026). Distances are straight lines between stations, scaled to each line's official length, so fares and times are estimates. Fares on some sections (such as Meguro–Shirokane-takanawa) are set separately. Always follow in-station signage and the official fare charts.
